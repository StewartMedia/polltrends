Why this call, and what could make it wrong

The sustained party-entity search leader will finish with the largest primary vote among tracked parties. This is an unvalidated hypothesis, not a calibrated election model.

A call needs a seven-day mean more than 10% above the runner-up, measured relative to the leader, and a daily lead on at least five of seven days. Observations older than three days withhold the current call. These thresholds are uncalibrated rules, not probabilities. Momentum and practical-voting research provide context, not adjustments to the prediction.

  • News or controversy can drive attention without support.
  • Search users are not a representative sample of voters.
  • National party topics filtered to Victoria can include federal political interest.
  • Leader and party indices use separate batches and cannot be compared as absolute volumes.

No seat totals, winning probabilities or government-formation prediction. Liberal and Nationals are separate parties here.

Intent percentages are shares of sampled related-query weight within each party, not search counts or vote shares. They contextualise but do not change the call.

Working theories

Rule-based readings of the data. Leads to test against polls and results, not findings.

  1. Labor searches lead to Liberal far more than the reverse. One reading: people starting from Labor are checking Liberal as the alternative.

    62% of Labor's top related searches name Liberal; 3% of Liberal's name Labor (this week).

  2. Labor searches lead to One Nation far more than the reverse. One reading: people starting from Labor are checking One Nation as the alternative.

    24% of Labor's top related searches name One Nation; 0% of One Nation's name Labor (this week).

  3. Prepoll research is showing up. Greens draws the most practical searches (how-to-vote cards, candidates and seats). That looks more like people planning a vote than general curiosity.

    How-to-vote and candidate searches as a share of each party's top related searches this week: Greens 18%, One Nation 17%.

  4. Minor parties are turning up in major-party searches. That points to preference and upper-house shopping, where minor parties matter most.

    Minor parties named in related searches this week: Family First (Liberal); Victorian Socialists (Greens, Liberal); Australian Democrats (Liberal); DLP (Greens).

How to read this page

Polls come from the public record collated on Wikipedia (snapshot 27 November 2026), each linked there to the original report. Seat margins are the 2022 two-candidate-preferred results from the published pre-election pendulum, with Prahran shown as a Liberal seat after the 2025 by-election. The uniform-swing model is the classic back-of-envelope read, not a forecast: real swings vary seat to seat, sitting members matter, and One Nation on 21.4% makes 2022 preference flows a weak guide. Search interest measures attention, not voting intention. Full method.
