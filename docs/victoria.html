Working theories

Rule-based readings of the data. Leads to test against polls and results, not findings.

  1. Labor searches lead to Liberal far more than the reverse. One reading: people starting from Labor are checking Liberal as the alternative.

    30% of Labor's top related searches name Liberal; 9% of Liberal's name Labor (this week).

  2. Prepoll research is showing up. One Nation draws the most practical searches (how-to-vote cards, candidates and seats). That looks more like people planning a vote than general curiosity.

    How-to-vote and candidate searches as a share of each party's top related searches this week: One Nation 13%, Liberal 12%.

  3. Minor parties are turning up in major-party searches. That points to preference and upper-house shopping, where minor parties matter most.

    Minor parties named in related searches this week: Family First (One Nation, Liberal, Greens); DLP (One Nation, Labor, Liberal, Greens); Australian Democrats (One Nation, Labor, Liberal, Greens); Citizens Party (Labor).

How to read this page

Polls come from the public record collated on Wikipedia (snapshot 23 November 2026), each linked there to the original report. Seat margins are the 2022 two-candidate-preferred results from the published pre-election pendulum, with Prahran shown as a Liberal seat after the 2025 by-election. The uniform-swing model is the classic back-of-envelope read, not a forecast: real swings vary seat to seat, sitting members matter, and One Nation on 21.3% makes 2022 preference flows a weak guide. Search interest measures attention, not voting intention. Full method.
